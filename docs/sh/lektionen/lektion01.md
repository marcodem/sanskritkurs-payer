At the beginning, a curiosity:


>"When William Jones [1746–1794] and Henry Thomas Colebrooke (1765—1857) first thoroughly studied Sanskrit, partially translated it and found that it had a rich literature and considerable kinship with the classical languages, they encountered no little resistance. Since this intimate relationship of Sanskrit to European languages so geographically distant could not be reconciled with the old views, which either derived all languages from Hebrew or largely isolated them from one another, the famous philologist Dugald Steward (1753—1828) took the simplest way out by declaring the entire history with the Sanskrit language a lie. He wrote an essay in which he tried to prove that it had been forged by the rascally Brahmins on the model of Greek and Latin, and that both the language and the literature were a forgery. This view was further elaborated in 1840 by the professor in Dublin, Charles William Wall (Göttingische gelehrte Anzeigen 1842 p. 1888)."

### 1.2.1. On the pronunciation of individual sounds

::: laut-table
| Sound | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **a - ⟪अ⟫** | "short a" is often pronounced as ə by Indians -- already for a long time. In Europe it is pronounced as short a, in Bengal as short dark o. |
| **ṛ - ⟪ऋ⟫** | like Czech vocalized r. Slight echo of i. |
| **ṝ - ⟪ॠ⟫** | like Czech vocalized r. Slight echo of u. |
| **jñ - ⟪ज्ञ्⟫** | also like dny (Marāṭhī) or gy (North Indian). |
| **ś - ⟪श्⟫** | sch-sound with the tip of the tongue bent downwards. Similar to sch in "mischen". |
| **ṣ - ⟪ष्⟫** | ach-sound with the tip of the tongue bent backwards. Often articulated so far back in the throat that it sounds almost like kh. |
| **h - ⟪ह्⟫** | aspirate, never a lengthening mark. |
| **ḥ - :** | **Visarga (Visarjanīya)** -- ⟪विसर्ग⟫ / ⟪विसर्जनीय⟫. Voiceless aspirate with the echo of the preceding vowel or the second part of the preceding diphthong: agniḥ -- ⟪अग्निः⟫ = agnihi, devaiḥ -- ⟪देवैः⟫ = devaihi, gauḥ -- ⟪गौः⟫ = gauhu |
| **ṃ** | **Anusvara** -- ⟪अनुस्वर⟫. Before sibilants, h, l: nasalization of the vowel. At the end of a word = m. In the middle of a word before consonants: the nasal corresponding to the following consonant: saṃdhi -- ⟪संधि⟫ = sandhi -- ⟪सन्धि⟫ |
:::
The best pronunciation is achieved when one reads the sentences, verses or words quite slowly and monotonously with exact consideration of the length of the vowels.
